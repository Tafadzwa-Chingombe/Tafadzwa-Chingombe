# Agency vs Freelancer vs DIY: Who Should Build Your Website?

**Short answer:** **DIY** website builders (Wix, WordPress.com, AI site builders) are the cheapest in cash but cost you time and skills. A **freelancer** can deliver excellent work at a lower price, as long as there's a written contract and a plan for aftercare. An **agency** costs more but gives you a team, process and ongoing support. Whoever you choose, ask three things before paying: **who owns the domain, is the price in writing, and who fixes the site after launch?**

There's no single right answer. It depends on your budget, your time, how important the website is to your sales, and how much support you need afterwards. Here's an honest comparison.

## Option 1: Do it yourself

**How it works:** you sign up for a website builder, choose a template, and add your own text and photos. Many now use AI to generate a first draft.

**Pros**
- Lowest cash cost to get started
- Full control; change things whenever you like
- Fast to launch a simple site

**Cons**
- It takes your time, often far more than expected
- Design, writing, photos and SEO all depend on your skills
- Local needs such as a .co.zw domain, EcoCash/Paynow payments and data protection basics can be harder to set up
- Monthly platform fees add up, and moving off the platform later can be difficult

**Best for:** very small budgets, simple one-page sites, or testing an idea before investing more.

## Option 2: Hire a freelancer

**How it works:** an independent designer or developer builds your site, often found through referrals, social media or freelance platforms.

**Pros**
- Usually lower prices than an agency
- Direct contact with the person doing the work
- Many freelancers are highly skilled

**Cons**
- One person means limited capacity: illness, travel or a full workload can delay your project
- Quality and reliability vary widely, so references matter
- Aftercare can be uncertain; some freelancers move on to other work or jobs
- The classic risk: the designer "disappears" with the deposit, or controls the domain

**Best for:** clearly defined projects with a modest budget, where you've seen the freelancer's past work and agreed everything in writing.

## Option 3: Work with an agency

**How it works:** a company with a team (designers, developers, writers, marketers) plans and builds your site and usually offers support afterwards.

**Pros**
- A team with a range of skills: design, development, content, SEO, ads
- Defined process, timelines and project management
- Continuity: if one person leaves, the company is still there
- Aftercare and support plans, plus help with marketing once you're live

**Cons**
- Higher price than most freelancers or DIY
- You may deal with an account manager rather than the person writing the code

**Best for:** businesses whose website is central to winning customers, organisations with procurement and compliance needs (corporates, NGOs, law firms, schools), and anyone who wants ongoing help with SEO and marketing.

## The 3 questions to ask before you pay anyone

### 1. Who owns the domain?
The domain (yourbusiness.co.zw) must be registered in your name or your company's name. If it's in the builder's name, you don't fully control your online address.

### 2. Is the price in writing?
Get a written quote listing pages, features, timeline, payment schedule and yearly costs. It protects you from surprise invoices. See [5 signs you're overpaying for your website](/blog/signs-you-are-overpaying-for-your-website).

### 3. Who fixes it after launch?
Websites need updates, backups and occasional fixes. Agree who handles them, how quickly, and at what cost, before the project starts. Ask whether you'll get a content manager so you can make simple changes yourself.

## What about AI website builders?

AI site builders can produce a decent-looking first draft in minutes, which is genuinely useful for testing an idea. But the words are generic, the structure may not match how Zimbabwean customers search, and local essentials still need a human: a .co.zw domain, EcoCash or Paynow payments, a Google Business Profile and data protection basics. Treat AI as a fast starting point, not a finished website.

## More smart questions

- Can I see three websites you've built, and speak to those clients?
- Will the site be fast on mobile data?
- Is basic SEO and a Google Business Profile included?
- Will I get logins for everything: domain, hosting, website admin?
- What happens to my site if we stop working together?

## Red flags in any website quote

Whether it comes from a freelancer or an agency, be careful if you see:

- **No written quote**, only a price on WhatsApp
- **100% payment upfront** for a large project, with no milestones
- **The domain registered in the builder's name**
- **No examples** of past work, or no clients you can speak to
- **Vague scope**, such as "a professional website" with no list of pages or features
- **No mention of aftercare**: who fixes problems, and at what cost
- **Promises of "guaranteed first place on Google"**. Nobody can guarantee rankings.

A good provider will be happy to put everything in writing, show you their work and explain what happens after launch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I hire an agency or a freelancer for my website?
Either can work. Choose a freelancer for smaller, clearly defined projects where you trust their track record; choose an agency when the website is central to your sales or you need a team, process and support.

### How do I know a web designer won't disappear with my deposit?
Check references and past work, sign a written agreement, pay in stages tied to milestones, and make sure the domain and hosting are in your name.

### Who owns my website and domain after it's built?
You should. Put ownership of the domain, content and design files in your contract, and make sure you receive all logins.

### Is Wix or WordPress good enough for a small business in Zimbabwe?
They can be, especially for simple sites. Check that local needs such as a .co.zw domain, EcoCash/Paynow payments and mobile speed are covered.

### How long does it take to build a website?
Simple sites can launch in days; larger sites with payments or many pages take weeks. A written timeline is part of a good quote.

### Can I start with DIY and move to an agency later?
Yes, many businesses do. Keep the domain in your own name from day one, keep copies of your text and photos, and choose a platform that lets you export your content. That makes it much easier to rebuild or move your site when you're ready to invest more.
