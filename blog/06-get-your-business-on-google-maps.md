# How to Get Your Business on Google Maps in Zimbabwe

**Short answer:** create a free **Google Business Profile**. Add your business name, category and location, verify that you own the business (usually by a short video recording), then complete the profile with photos, opening hours, your phone number and your website. Once it's verified, your business can appear on Google Maps and in local search results, with buttons for directions and calls.

When someone in Harare, Bulawayo or Mutare searches "pharmacy near me" or "car repairs Eastlea", Google shows a map with a short list of businesses at the top of the results. Being in that list is one of the most valuable, and cheapest, ways to get new customers. It costs nothing to set up.

## Why Google Maps matters for local businesses

- **People search with intent.** Someone searching "plumber near me" needs a plumber now.
- **The map comes first.** Local results often appear above ordinary website results.
- **One tap to call or visit.** Customers can call you, get directions or open WhatsApp and your website straight from the listing.
- **Reviews build trust.** Star ratings and reviews help people choose between you and a competitor.
- **It's free.** Google Business Profile has no cost.

## Step 1: Open Google Business Profile

Go to Google Business Profile (business.google.com) and sign in with a Google account. Use an account the business controls, such as your business Gmail, not a personal account belonging to one staff member or your web designer.

## Step 2: Add your name, category and location

- **Business name:** your real business name as it appears on your signage. Don't add keywords ("Best Plumber Harare Cheap"), which breaks Google's rules and can get your profile suspended.
- **Category:** choose the most specific primary category (for example "Law firm" rather than "Consultant"). You can add secondary categories later.
- **Location:** if customers visit you, add your address and drop the pin accurately. If you serve customers at their homes (plumbers, electricians, cleaners), you can hide your address and set the areas you serve instead.

## Step 3: Verify your business

Google needs to confirm you're a real business at that location. Verification options depend on your business, but many businesses are now asked to **record a short video** on their phone through the verification screen. Expect to show:

- your location (street signs or nearby landmarks)
- your business in operation (equipment, stock or your workspace)
- proof of management (unlocking your premises, or business documents)

Record in one continuous, unedited take and upload from your phone. Reviews commonly take a few business days, sometimes longer. Don't create duplicate profiles while you wait.

## Step 4: Complete your profile

A complete profile ranks better and converts more searchers into customers:

- **Phone number:** the number customers should call. Make sure someone answers.
- **Website:** link to your website so people can learn more and buy.
- **Opening hours:** including public holidays.
- **Photos:** your storefront, interior, team, products and work. Real photos beat stock images.
- **Services or products:** list what you offer, with prices where possible.
- **Description:** a plain-English summary of what you do and where.

**Tip for Zimbabwean addresses:** many streets and suburbs are well known locally but harder for Google to place. Double-check that your map pin sits exactly on your building, and mention a well-known landmark nearby in your description ("opposite the Eastlea shopping centre", for example) so first-time visitors can find you easily.

## Step 5: Get reviews and keep it active

- **Ask happy customers for reviews** and send them the direct review link from your profile.
- **Reply to every review**, good and bad, politely and quickly.
- **Post updates** on offers, new products and news. Activity signals that the business is open and engaged.
- **Keep hours and details up to date**, especially if you move or change phone numbers.

## How to ask for reviews (without feeling awkward)

Most happy customers are willing to leave a review. They just need to be asked at the right moment, with an easy link. Try a message like this on WhatsApp after a successful job or delivery:

> "Thank you for choosing us! If you were happy with our service, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It really helps other customers find us: [your review link]."

Ask soon after the customer has had a good experience, keep it short, and never offer payment or discounts in exchange for reviews; that is against Google's rules.

## Local SEO beyond Google Maps

Your Google Business Profile is the foundation of local SEO, but a few other steps help you rank and get chosen:

- **Consistent details everywhere:** use the same business name, address and phone number on your website, Facebook page, directories and Google profile.
- **Location pages on your website:** if you serve several areas, a page for each, with real information, helps you appear in those searches.
- **Business directories:** list your business on reputable local directories and industry associations.
- **Fresh content:** helpful blog articles that answer customers' questions show Google you're active and knowledgeable.

## Common mistakes

- Using a home address publicly when you don't serve customers there.
- Stuffing keywords into the business name.
- Creating several profiles for the same location.
- Letting a former employee or designer own the profile, so nobody can update it later.
- Ignoring reviews and questions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Google Business Profile free?
Yes. Creating and managing a Google Business Profile costs nothing.

### How long does Google Business Profile verification take?
It varies. Video verification reviews commonly take a few business days, but can take longer. Record one continuous video and avoid editing it.

### Can I list my business on Google Maps without a shop?
Yes. Service-area businesses can hide their address and show the areas they serve instead.

### Why can't I find my business on Google?
It may not be verified, may be in the wrong category, or may be outranked by more complete profiles with more reviews. Complete every section and ask customers for reviews.

### Do I still need a website if I'm on Google Maps?
Yes. Your profile gets attention; your website gives people the detail and confidence to contact or buy from you.

### Can someone else manage my Google Business Profile for me?
Yes. You can add managers to your profile, such as your marketing agency, without giving away ownership. Always keep the business itself as the primary owner, so you never lose control if you change providers.
