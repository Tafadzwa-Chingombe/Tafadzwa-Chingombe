# Is Your Website POTRAZ-Ready? A Data Protection Checklist

> **General information, not legal advice.** This article explains how Zimbabwe's data protection rules affect business websites in practical terms. For decisions about licensing and legal obligations, speak to a qualified lawyer or contact POTRAZ.

**Short answer:** if your website, contact form, newsletter or customer list collects personal data (names, phone numbers, email addresses), Zimbabwe's Cyber and Data Protection Act applies to you. Under Statutory Instrument 155 of 2024, organisations processing personal data of **50 or more people** generally need a **data controller licence** from POTRAZ, and POTRAZ announced mandatory compliance inspections from **1 September 2026**. For your website, start with a privacy policy, consent on your forms, a secure HTTPS site and a clear record of where data is stored.

Data protection used to feel like something only banks and telecoms worried about. Not any more. A simple "Contact us" form or a WhatsApp list of customers is personal data, and it brings small businesses, schools, churches and NGOs into scope.

## What the rules say (in plain English)

- **The law:** the Cyber and Data Protection Act (Chapter 12:07) sets out how personal information must be collected, used, stored and protected in Zimbabwe.
- **The regulations:** Statutory Instrument 155 of 2024 (the Cyber and Data Protection (Licensing of Data Controllers and Appointment of Data Protection Officers) Regulations) sets out licensing and the appointment of data protection officers.
- **Who needs a licence:** organisations that process personal data of 50 or more individuals generally need a data controller licence from POTRAZ, which acts as the Data Protection Authority. Licence tiers and fees depend on how many people's data you process.
- **Data Protection Officer:** licensed controllers are expected to appoint a data protection officer.
- **Inspections:** POTRAZ issued a regulatory notice announcing mandatory, risk-based compliance inspections of data controllers from 1 September 2026.
- **Breaches:** reported guidance says breaches must be reported to POTRAZ quickly, within 24 hours, and affected people notified within 72 hours.

Fees, tiers and deadlines can change. Check the current regulations and POTRAZ notices, and take legal advice for your specific situation.

## Does my website collect personal data?

Probably. Personal data is any information that identifies a person. On a typical business website that includes:

- **Contact and enquiry forms:** names, phone numbers, email addresses, messages
- **Newsletter sign-ups:** email addresses
- **Online shops and bookings:** names, addresses, phone numbers, order history
- **Job application forms:** CVs and personal details
- **Analytics and cookies:** IP addresses and device information
- **Linked WhatsApp lists and customer databases** your website feeds into

If your organisation holds this kind of information about 50 or more people, the licensing rules are likely to apply.

## Your website data protection checklist

### 1. Publish a privacy policy
A clear, plain-English page explaining what data you collect, why, how long you keep it, who you share it with, and how people can ask to see or delete their data. Link it in your website footer and next to every form.

### 2. Get consent on your forms
Add a short line and an unticked checkbox to forms: "I agree that [your business] may use my details to respond to my enquiry, as described in the privacy policy." For newsletters, ask for separate, explicit consent.

### 3. Collect only what you need
If a phone number is enough, don't ask for an ID number or home address. Less data means less risk.

### 4. Secure the website with HTTPS
Your site should load with https:// and a padlock. It encrypts form submissions between the visitor and your server.

### 5. Know where the data is stored
Do form submissions go to an email inbox, a database, a spreadsheet or a third-party service? Where is the server? Who has access? Write it down. Inspectors and your DPO will ask.

### 6. Limit access and keep software updated
Give staff access only to the data they need. Keep your website, plugins and content manager updated, and use strong passwords with two-step verification.

### 7. Have a breach plan
Decide who does what if data is leaked or a site is hacked, including who notifies POTRAZ and affected people, and how quickly.

### 8. Check your suppliers
Your web host, email provider, payment gateway and marketing tools all handle your customers' data. Make sure they're reputable and that your contracts cover data protection.

## Example: consent wording for your contact form

Place this directly above your form's submit button, with an unticked checkbox:

> ☐ I agree that [Business name] may use the details I've provided to respond to my enquiry, as explained in our [Privacy Policy]. I can ask for my details to be deleted at any time.

For a newsletter, use a separate checkbox:

> ☐ Yes, I'd like to receive occasional emails with news and offers. I can unsubscribe at any time.

## What to include in a website privacy policy

Keep it in plain English. A typical privacy policy covers:

1. **Who you are** and how to contact you (and your Data Protection Officer, if appointed)
2. **What data you collect**, such as names, phone numbers, emails, order details and website analytics
3. **Why you collect it**: to respond to enquiries, deliver orders or send newsletters with consent
4. **Who you share it with**, such as your web host, payment gateway or delivery partners
5. **How long you keep it**, and how it is stored securely
6. **People's rights**: to access, correct or delete their data, and how to ask
7. **Cookies and analytics** used on the website
8. **Updates to the policy**, with the date it was last changed

Have a lawyer review the final wording, especially if you handle sensitive data such as health, financial or children's information.

## Who is affected?

Most organisations that deal with the public: SMEs, retailers, schools, churches, clinics, NGOs, professional firms and membership organisations. Larger organisations, and those handling sensitive data such as health or financial records, are likely to face closer scrutiny.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a POTRAZ data controller licence for my website?
If your organisation processes personal data of 50 or more people, through your website or otherwise, you are likely to need one. Confirm with POTRAZ or a lawyer.

### Does a contact form count as collecting personal data?
Yes. Names, phone numbers and email addresses submitted through a form are personal data.

### What does a Data Protection Officer do?
A DPO oversees how your organisation handles personal data, keeps records, advises staff, and is the contact point for POTRAZ and the public on data protection matters.

### Does my website need a privacy policy?
Yes, if it collects personal data. A privacy policy explains what you collect and why, and how people can exercise their rights.

### When did POTRAZ start inspections?
POTRAZ announced mandatory compliance inspections of data controllers from 1 September 2026.
