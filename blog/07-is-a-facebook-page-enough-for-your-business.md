# Is a Facebook Page Enough for Your Business? Facebook Page vs Website

**Short answer:** a Facebook page is a great place to talk to customers, but it isn't enough on its own. It's rented space: Meta decides who sees your posts, it rarely ranks on Google for the services you sell, and it can't run a proper checkout with EcoCash or card payments. A website is an asset you own. Keep your Facebook page for conversation and community, and use a website to be found, build trust and close sales.

"I have a Facebook page, I don't need a website." We hear this from small business owners across Zimbabwe, and it's understandable. Facebook is free, your customers are there, and posting is easy. Millions of Zimbabweans use social media, so why spend money on a website?

Here's a fair look at both.

## What a Facebook page does well

- **Reach and engagement.** People already scroll Facebook every day.
- **Conversation.** Comments and Messenger make it easy for customers to ask questions.
- **Social proof.** Likes, shares and reviews show you're active and trusted.
- **Low cost to start.** A page is free, and ads can start on a small budget.

For a brand-new business testing an idea, a Facebook page (plus WhatsApp) is a sensible first step.

## Where a Facebook page falls short

### You don't own it
Your page lives on Meta's platform, under Meta's rules. Pages can be restricted, hacked or lost, and policy changes can cut your reach overnight. Everything you've built sits on land you're renting.

### The algorithm decides who sees you
Even people who follow your page often don't see your posts. Organic reach is limited, and getting in front of more people usually means paying for ads.

### It isn't built to rank on Google
When someone searches "accountant in Harare" or "school uniforms Bulawayo", Google mostly shows websites and Google Business Profiles. Your Facebook page may appear when people search your exact business name, but rarely for the services you sell.

### It can't run a proper checkout
Customers can't pay you with EcoCash, OneMoney or Visa through a normal Facebook page checkout in Zimbabwe. Payment ends up in DMs and screenshots.

### It looks less established to some buyers
Corporate clients, NGOs, diaspora customers and anyone spending serious money often look for a website before they trust a business. "We only have a Facebook page" can lose you the bigger deals.

## Facebook page vs website at a glance

| | Facebook page | Your website |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns it | Meta | You |
| Found on Google for your services | Rarely | Yes, with good SEO |
| Who sees your content | The algorithm decides | Anyone who visits |
| Online payments (EcoCash, Visa) | No | Yes, with a gateway such as Paynow |
| Full product and service details | Limited | Unlimited |
| Professional credibility | Good for small sales | Stronger for larger buyers |

### Your followers aren't your customers yet
A large following feels like success, but followers only become customers when they can easily find your prices, trust you and take the next step. A website is where that happens: the place your posts, ads and WhatsApp chats can point to, with everything a buyer needs to decide in one place.

## The best answer: use both

The strongest small businesses use social media and a website together:

1. **Facebook and Instagram** attract attention and start conversations.
2. **Your website** gives the full details: services, prices, reviews and location.
3. **WhatsApp** closes the sale with personal service.
4. **Online payments** on the website take money without the back-and-forth.

Link your website in your Facebook page's "About" section and in posts, and add your social links to your website. Read more in [Website + WhatsApp: how they work together](/blog/website-and-whatsapp-for-business-zimbabwe).

## Moving from Facebook-only to a website: a 5-step plan

1. **List your top questions.** Write down the questions customers ask most on Facebook and WhatsApp. These become your website's pages and FAQs.
2. **Start small.** A fast, mobile-friendly site with your services, prices or ranges, photos, reviews and a WhatsApp button is enough to begin.
3. **Claim your Google Business Profile.** Link it to your new website so you start appearing in local searches. See [How to get your business on Google Maps](/blog/get-your-business-on-google-maps-zimbabwe).
4. **Point your followers to the website.** Pin a post, add the link to your page's About section and include it in replies.
5. **Measure.** Track website visits and WhatsApp clicks, and add pages for whatever people ask about most.

## A real-world scenario

A beauty salon has 8,000 Facebook followers but gets most new bookings through referrals. When people search "nail salon Borrowdale" on Google, three competitors with websites and Google profiles appear; the salon doesn't. After adding a simple website with prices, a gallery and a booking button, plus a Google Business Profile with reviews, the salon starts showing up for local searches. Facebook keeps followers engaged, and the website catches the customers who were searching but never saw its posts.

## When is a Facebook page enough?

A Facebook page alone can be enough if:

- you're testing a new idea with a very small budget
- you sell mostly to people who already know you
- you don't need to be found on Google by strangers

You need a website when you want new customers from search, bigger clients, online payments or a brand that isn't at the mercy of one platform.

## What should a small business website include?

At minimum: what you do, who you serve, where you are, prices or price ranges, real photos, reviews, and a clear call and WhatsApp button on every page. It should load fast on mobile data. ## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a website if I have a Facebook page?
If you want to be found on Google, take online payments, win larger clients or own your online presence, yes. A Facebook page alone works mainly for people who already know you.

### Does a Facebook page show up on Google?
Sometimes, for searches of your exact business name. It rarely ranks for the services or products you sell, which is how most new customers search.

### Is a website expensive to maintain?
Running costs are mainly the yearly domain, hosting and email, plus optional support. See [How much does a website cost in Zimbabwe?](/blog/how-much-does-a-website-cost-in-zimbabwe).

### Can I take EcoCash payments through Facebook?
Not through a standard Facebook page checkout. A website connected to a payment gateway such as Paynow can take EcoCash and card payments.

### Will a website replace my Facebook marketing?
No. They do different jobs. Keep posting on Facebook and Instagram to stay visible and start conversations, and use your website as the place where interested people get the full picture, see your prices and contact or pay you. Each one makes the other more effective.
